# Shipping

## Core thesis

The global scramble for ports is a reflection of geopolitical anxieties, particularly regarding China's influence.

## Summary

The global competition for port control is intensifying, driven by geopolitical tensions, particularly between the U.S. and China. Investments in port infrastructure are expected to rise significantly, but this rush may lead to inefficiencies and political pressures affecting shipping routes. China's dominance in global ports is causing concern among Western nations, prompting them to bolster their own maritime capabilities.

## Key entities

- **COSCO** (Company): Owner. Chinese state-owned firm that owns a majority stake in the Port of Piraeus.
- **Hapag-Lloyd** (Company): Acquirer. German shipping giant involved in acquiring stakes in container-terminal operators.
- **AP Moller-Maersk** (Company): Investor. Shipping giant involved in expanding terminal operations in Europe.
- **BlackRock** (Company): Investor. American asset manager involved in acquiring non-Chinese ports.
- **Federal Maritime Commission (FMC)** (Government Agency): Regulator. U.S. agency overseeing maritime shipping and protecting American interests.
